How to use Booking

A shared calendar only works if people book before they use something, and give the time back when they do not. This page covers booking, finding a free slot, tagging the people with you, changing and cancelling, and — for lab admins — adding a room or instrument and setting its rules. What Booking is →

Golden rules

  1. Book before you use it, not after. A calendar that is filled in afterwards tells nobody anything while it matters.
  2. Give the time back. Cancel as soon as you know you are not coming; the slot is no use to anyone while it is still yours.
  3. Book what you need, not the whole day. Say what it is for in the title, so someone who needs the instrument knows whether to ask.
  4. Tag the people with you. The booking then shows on their calendar and their notebook day too.
  5. Say what it is for in the title. Genotyping T2 (35S-F / tRFP-R) is useful; booking is not.

Booking something

  1. Calendar: pick the room or instrument at the left. Its week appears, with today marked.
  2. Click an empty slot, or drag down it for a longer one. Use the arrows to change week, or the date box to jump.
  3. Give it a title saying what it is for, check the start and end, and add anyone else taking part.
  4. Save. It appears in the calendar in that instrument's colour, and under My bookings.

Where several things of one kind are shared — four growth chambers, say — All <kind>s shows them side by side for one day, which is the quickest way to see what is free.

Finding a free slot

When what matters is getting an hour on a microscope rather than a particular one, use Find a slot:

  1. Say the kind of thing you need, and where, if it matters.
  2. Say how long for, and roughly when — a date range, and the part of the day that suits.
  3. It lists the free slots that fit, across everything of that kind you may book. Pick one and it is filled in for you.

The people with you

Add whoever is taking part to the booking. For each of them it then appears under My bookings and on their notebook day, so a session with a student or a visitor is on everybody's calendar without a second booking.

Tagging someone does not let them change the booking; it stays with whoever made it, and with the instrument's managers.

Changing and cancelling

  • My bookings lists what is coming, soonest first.
  • Open one to change its time, its title or who is in it — the same rules are checked again.
  • Cancel frees the slot at once. A cancelled booking leaves the calendar but is kept, so it is clear what happened.
  • A booking that has already started can be ended early by shortening it.

When a booking is refused

The calendar says which rule it ran into:

  • "booked in steps of 30 minutes" — start and finish on the step, not at 10:17.
  • "booked for at least…" / "at most…" — the shortest and longest allowed for that instrument.
  • "can be booked at most N days ahead" — wait, or ask one of its managers, who are not held to that limit.
  • "is not booked on Sundays" / "booked from 08:00 to 18:00" — its open days and hours.
  • "that time has passed" — the slot has gone; book from now on.
  • A clash — someone has it. Their booking is on the calendar, with who made it, so you can ask.

Your notebook

Open a day in the Lab Notebook and the bookings you had that day are at the top, with the instrument and the time, and what is coming next. Nothing to fill in: it is simply there, so a picture in the notebook can be traced to the microscope that took it.

Adding a room or instrument

For a lab's lab admins. Under Manage:

  1. Add, then its name and what kind of thing it is — Meeting room, Microscope, Growth chamber. The kind is free text, but keep to the words already in use: Find a slot and the day view group by it.
  2. Where it is, how many it seats if that matters, and a colour so it is recognisable in the calendar.
  3. In the description, put what people need to know before they book: the rules of the room, who to ask for training, where the key is.
  4. Set its rules and who it is shared with, below.

Something no longer in use is retired rather than deleted: it leaves the lists, and the bookings that were made on it stay readable.

Setting the rules

  • Steps — 15, 30 or 60 minutes, or a whole day. Bookings must start and end on these.
  • Shortest and longest — stops both a five-minute booking and a week-long one. Leave the longest empty for no limit.
  • How far ahead — the usual way of keeping a popular instrument fair. Its managers may book beyond it, for courses and service visits.
  • Open days and hours — which weekdays, and between what times. A growth chamber is normally open always; a meeting room is not.

Changing a rule does not disturb bookings already made.

Sharing it

Choose who may see and book it: your own lab, named labs, whole chair groups, or everyone in the suite. Anyone it is shared with can book it; only its managers change its rules or move someone else's booking.

Fixing mistakes

  • Booked the wrong instrument: cancel it and book the right one. There is no penalty for cancelling early, and every reason to.
  • Booked the wrong week: open the booking and change its date rather than making a second one.
  • Someone has a slot they are not using: their name is on the booking. Ask them; a manager can move it if they cannot be reached.
  • An instrument is missing from the list: it may not be shared with your lab yet. Ask the lab that owns it.
  • The rules are wrong for how the lab works: a lab admin can change them under Manage, and existing bookings are left alone.
